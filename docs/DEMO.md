# Two-minute museum walkthrough

Read at a conversational pace and pause while each exhibit opens. Bracketed directions are screen actions.

**0:00–0:15 · Hero**  
[Start on the homepage and open the archive.] What happens to a space machine when the people who sent it can no longer drive it, listen to it, or bring it home? This museum lets us visit five of those machines. Let’s start with one that once had a driver.

**0:15–0:37 · Moon**  
[Choose Moon and open the Apollo 15 rover.] Imagine trying to visit a distant patch of rock on foot in a spacesuit. This electric buggy carried astronauts and their tools around the landing site. The astronauts brought samples home. The buggy stayed on the Moon. Its wheels are still there because its job was to help people explore the surface.

**0:37–1:02 · Mars**  
[Close the file, switch to Mars, open InSight.] Here’s a different way to explore. InSight stayed in one place. Its seismometer felt tiny movements in the ground. Waves from a marsquake travel through different materials on their way to the instrument. Scientists used those vibrations to investigate layers deep inside Mars. A child looking at the ground beneath the lander can ask what the instrument could sense that a camera could never see.

**1:02–1:24 · Destination explorer and timeline**  
[Choose deep space in the explorer. Show Voyager 1 and its timeline entry.] Voyager 1 gives us another ending to consider. Its encounters with Jupiter and Saturn are history, but the probe kept going. In 2012 it crossed into interstellar space. Find that event on the timeline and compare it with the year Voyager launched. How long did that part of the journey take?

**1:24–1:45 · Activity**  
[Open the quiz. Read a question aloud, let the audience guess, then choose an answer.] What piece of evidence helped you decide? We can return to the exhibit and follow its NASA sources to check. That’s the same habit researchers practice when they ask how a measurement supports a conclusion.

**1:45–2:00 · Closing**  
[Show the closing section and source links.] The next time you hear that a space mission has ended, try asking two more questions. Where is the machine now? And what can someone still discover from what it sent home?

## Short cut · approximately 25 seconds

A buggy parked on the Moon, a silent Mars lander and a probe traveling through interstellar space have very different stories. Space Explorers: Where Are They Now? lets young explorers compare five missions through the hardware, the evidence and the people who made sense of it. Choose a destination, follow a mission file back to NASA’s sources and test what you learned.
