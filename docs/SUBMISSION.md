## One-sentence description

Explore five NASA missions through the hardware they left behind, from a parked Moon buggy to a probe still traveling between the stars.

## Project description · copy text

What can a child learn from a rover whose wheels will never turn again? In Space Explorers: Where Are They Now?, visitors explore five museum exhibits about the machines that helped us investigate other worlds. Open Opportunity’s file and examine the evidence that water once changed Martian rock. Visit InSight to discover how vibrations can reveal the inside of a planet. On the Moon, compare a buggy parked after carrying astronauts with a spacecraft deliberately sent into a crater. Then follow Voyager 1 beyond the region dominated by the solar wind.

Each exhibit pairs the hardware’s fate with a scientific question, the observations that helped answer it and links to the NASA record. Children can choose a destination, compare events on a timeline and test an idea in the quiz. Families and classrooms can open the museum on a phone or computer without creating an account.

## Challenge alignment

The brief asks children to meet hardware left on the Moon and Mars and understand the science it enabled. The Apollo 15 rover, LCROSS, Opportunity and InSight anchor those two worlds. Voyager 1 supplies a comparison in deep space. A parked crew vehicle, a planned impact, loss of contact and an extended mission each tell a different kind of ending. ## Educational impact

A student can compare two missions by answering three questions. What did the instrument measure? What conclusion did its scientists draw? What happened to the hardware? For example, Opportunity examined rock while InSight sensed vibrations traveling through the ground. Both visited Mars, yet they answered different questions about the planet. The quiz provides a chance to recall the distinction, and the NASA links give students a way to check an answer. These are learning objectives. Classroom outcomes have yet to be measured.

## Innovation

The museum lets a visitor choose an exhibit by its ending. A child who expects every machine to have broken down can put the Apollo rover beside LCROSS and explain why parking one and crashing the other both accomplished what mission planners intended. Moving between a destination, a dated event and a mission file offers several entry points into the same evidence.

## Technology and provenance

The site uses HTML, CSS, browser JavaScript and locally packaged exhibit assets. Its curated mission records supply the archive, rather than a live spacecraft feed. NASA mission records and credited imagery provide the scientific source material. OpenAI ChatGPT/Codex assisted with research, writing exhibit text and documentation, generating code and illustrative artwork, and testing this pre-event educational build. Source links distinguish NASA’s published evidence from exhibit interpretation.
